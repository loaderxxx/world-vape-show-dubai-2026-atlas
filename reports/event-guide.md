# Beyond the exhibitor list
## A reader's guide to World Vape Show Dubai 2026

**For readers who were not there · About 8 minutes · Research cut-off: 10 November 2026**

The most useful way to understand this exhibition is not as a row of product brands. The published programme and exhibitor descriptions reveal a wider industrial system: consumer devices, chemical ingredients, batteries, manufacturing, packaging, testing, distribution and regulation. A recognizable name on a stand is often only the visible part of that system.

This guide connects those parts. It is a source-based explanation of the event's announced scope, not an eyewitness report. The research was assembled on the opening date, before the three-day exhibition had finished. It does not claim to know which sessions actually took place, which products were launched, what visitors thought, or what agreements were signed.

**Reading route:** [The event](#1-the-event-in-context) → [The participants](#2-a-conceptual-walk-through-the-exhibition) → [The programme](#3-what-the-forum-was-set-up-to-discuss) → [The conclusions](#5-what-a-non-attendee-can-reasonably-take-away).

## 1. The event in context

World Vape Show Dubai is a professional trade exhibition. The organizer lists the 2026 edition for **10–12 November at Dubai World Trade Centre**, across the Arena, Hall 1 and Za'abeel Hall 3, with admission restricted to people aged 21 or older. Its conference component is the Future Nicotine Forum. These are organizer-published details, not independently observed attendance facts. [Visitor information](https://worldvapeshow.com/dubai/visiting/visitor-faqs).

An important detail for understanding the catalogue: WVS is co-located with **WT Middle East and World Cigar Show**. The WT organizer identifies 10–11 November for its own event. That explains why the official PDF contains tobacco-processing and cigar material as well as the WVS pages. Co-location does not make the three exhibitor lists identical, and their dates and opening hours should not be treated as interchangeable. [Co-location announcement and catalogue landing page](https://wtprocessandmachinery.com/middle-east).

The atlas deliberately covers the WVS list, not every participant across the combined events. The website supplies 138 entries; reconciling it with the catalogue produces 140 distinct names. Those figures describe the sources reviewed, not verified attendance, exhibition scale or the number of independent legal companies.

## 2. A conceptual walk through the exhibition

The sections below are a reading aid, not a physical route or a floor-plan reconstruction. Examples explain different roles; they are not recommendations, rankings or claims of market leadership. Each linked profile contains sources and limitations.

### The visible layer: brands and devices

Names such as [GEEK VAPE, Jwei Group and Lost Vape](../profiles/02-foom-nysa.md), and [SMOK, Vaporesso and VooPoo](../profiles/03-oplus-zinwi.md), represent the device-facing side of the list. Their reviewed material discusses electronic atomization hardware, device families or related engineering.

Even here, a brand and a manufacturer are not necessarily the same thing. Some entries describe corporate groups; others are consumer-facing names. A branded device does not by itself identify the company responsible for every component, the factory assembling it, or the legal entity exhibiting it.

### Behind the brand: engineering and manufacturing

[ALD, Cellular Workshop and FIRST UNION](../profiles/01-aaok-flavoriq.md) describe engineering or manufacturing activities. [Moshvape/Thinkvape](../profiles/02-foom-nysa.md) is itself a combined-name entry with an unresolved legal relationship.

This layer explains why a public exhibitor list is not a clean company database. Brand development, design, assembly and ownership can sit in different organizations. The atlas records what the reviewed source says without silently resolving those relationships.

### Ingredients and formulation

[Alchem NicSelect, Adroit Naturals and FLAVORIQ](../profiles/01-aaok-flavoriq.md) describe ingredient or formulation activities. [QUAD Lifesciences and ZINWI](../profiles/03-oplus-zinwi.md) illustrate additional chemical and formulation roles.

Their presence broadens the subject beyond finished hardware. However, descriptions such as pharmaceutical grade, certified or laboratory tested require document-level scrutiny. The atlas has not validated the relevant standards, test methods, production sites or product approvals.

### Products that are not electronic devices

The list also includes oral nicotine names such as [BAGZ, CLEW and FEDRS](../profiles/01-aaok-flavoriq.md). This matters analytically: the event's name does not mean that every participant makes an electronic cigarette.

Different product categories should not be combined into a single technical or regulatory category. Nor should an exhibitor's use of terms such as tobacco-free be treated as a statement that a nicotine product is harmless.

### Infrastructure that is easy to overlook

[BATTSYS](../profiles/01-aaok-flavoriq.md) and [Huizhou Hongtai](../profiles/02-foom-nysa.md) describe batteries. [Packmate and Sidsam Formilan](../profiles/03-oplus-zinwi.md) describe packaging machinery, although the short Packmate exhibitor name still needs a stronger legal-identity match. Queen Global Logistics has an organizer description of logistics services.

These are different kinds of businesses from the finished-product brands. Their inclusion makes the list informative about the exhibition's industrial breadth, but it does not prove any particular supply relationship among the companies present.

### Evidence, identity and accountability

[Cypheme](../profiles/01-aaok-flavoriq.md) describes product authentication; [National Laboratory & Research](../profiles/02-foom-nysa.md) describes testing; [Tamarind Intelligence](../profiles/03-oplus-zinwi.md) describes market and regulatory research. Their roles show why the event cannot be understood solely through product catalogues.

An authentication claim, a laboratory description and a market report are also three different types of evidence. None automatically establishes that a particular product is lawful, safe or accurately represented.

## 3. What the forum was set up to discuss

The detailed programme places the **Future Nicotine Forum on 10–11 November in the Al Mutaqua Ballroom**. Its first-day schedule includes a MENA market session with Euromonitor's Alexandra Molokova, followed later by discussion of regulation and supply chains. That panel brings together policy, regulatory and industry perspectives, including Inter Scientific, Tamarind Intelligence and Indonesian customs and industry representatives. These are announced roles, not confirmation of attendance or a summary of remarks delivered. [Published programme](https://worldvapeshow.com/dubai/sessions/).

The printed programme also includes sessions about device design, scientific evidence, sustainability and regional tobacco production. That breadth is consistent with the combined-event setting. Session descriptions are statements of intended topics, not findings of scientific consensus. [Official catalogue, PDF pages 33–34 and 37](https://wt-process.files.svdcdn.com/production/misc/wtme26-show-catalogue.pdf?dm=1793619240).

There is a scheduling inconsistency: the FAQ gives 10–12 November for the forum, while the detailed agenda gives 10–11 November. This guide preserves that difference. A careful account should not turn an outdated schedule into a claim about what occurred.

## 4. What the research uncovered

The source discrepancies are part of the story, not clerical footnotes.

**The same count can conceal a different list.** Both sources contain 138 names, but A-Sense and FeelmixX appear only in the catalogue, while Fox Electronics and THE GOLD LOTUS appear only on the live website. FeelmixX and THE GOLD LOTUS share a published stand number across the two sources; that is not enough evidence to merge them.

**A location can change depending on the source.** Vape Dinner Lady is at 1220 online but at The Pavilion in print.

**A profile can describe the wrong apparent identity.** Ayla/Tesiyi's organizer page discusses Wafoo. LVL's page discusses Aspire. Those conflicts are retained in the profiles instead of being converted into confident company histories.

**Several names can occupy one stand.** Four website entries use 3430; three other stand numbers each have two entries. This supports a warning about double-counting, not a conclusion about ownership.

See the [source reconciliation](source-reconciliation.md) for the exact differences and [cross-analysis](cross-analysis.md) for their implications.

## 5. What a non-attendee can reasonably take away

### The event's scope is wider than its title

The list spans devices, oral products, chemistry, components, machinery, testing and research. That conclusion follows from the reviewed participant descriptions. It does not require a claim about which sector dominated the actual exhibition floor.

### Company identity is a central research problem

Display names mix brands, groups, legal names and paired labels. The more polished the presentation, the easier it is to overlook that distinction. The atlas therefore keeps the organizer's name, source, evidence type and unresolved identity questions visible.

### Regulatory discussion is prominent in the announced programme

The agenda explicitly gives space to regulation, scientific evidence and product responsibility. This establishes editorial emphasis in the programme. It does not establish the quality of the discussion, the independence of every speaker or a shared position among participants.

### The most honest result is a bounded picture

The atlas has a record for all 140 names in the reconciled set. Six remain listing-only; most other descriptions rely on companies or organizers. No full legal-identity, financial, certification or product-safety audit was completed.

There are no verified 2026 attendance totals, visitor interviews, on-site photographs, deal announcements or independently checked product-launch findings in this project. Those would require a separate evidence layer before this guide could become a post-show report.

## A short glossary

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| Brand | The name used to identify a product or business to its audience; not necessarily a legal company. |
| Legal entity | The registered organization that can hold rights and obligations. |
| OEM / ODM | Manufacturing arrangements involving another company's product or design; exact responsibilities depend on the arrangement. |
| Pod system | A device architecture using a cartridge or pod. |
| E-liquid | Liquid intended for use in an electronic vaping device; composition must be established for the specific product. |
| Nicotine pouch | An oral product containing nicotine; distinct from an electronic device. |
| Notification | A submission to an authority; not necessarily an approval or safety endorsement. |
| Accreditation | Recognition of competence within a specified scope; the scope and current status matter. |
| Self-description | Information a company publishes about itself; useful for orientation but not independent validation. |
