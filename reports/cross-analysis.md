# Cross-analysis: what the evidence supports

**Scope:** 138 live-list entries plus 2 catalogue-only entries. This is first-level desk research, not corporate due diligence or a procurement assessment.

## 1. Coverage is complete; verification is not

All 140 named entries have a research record containing an activity description or explicit unknown, source links and unresolved questions. Every live-list entry has a stand/profile reference preserved. **No exhibitor's legal identity has been independently verified end to end.** Brand pages and organizer descriptions usually cannot establish the legal entity operating a stand.

| Principal evidence group | Records |
|---|---:|
| Company or brand self-description used | 94 |
| Organizer description used | 19 |
| Limited or ambiguous identity evidence | 16 |
| Listing only | 6 |
| Corporate or regulatory record used | 5 |
| Total | 140 |

These are research labels, not quality scores. The grouping derives from the detailed verification_level field. A record placed in the company-source group can still have an uncertain identity match; always read its individual gaps. Regulatory filings, product notifications and trademark entries do not establish product safety or authorization in every market.

## 2. The exhibition is not one uniform company category

Reviewed descriptions cover several distinct activities:

- Finished-device and consumer-product brands.
- Contract manufacturing and engineering.
- Ingredients and formulation.
- Oral nicotine and tobacco-related products.
- Batteries and electronic components.
- Packaging machinery and logistics.
- Laboratory testing, authentication technology, associations and market research.

Examples of the distinction: BATTSYS and Huizhou Hongtai describe batteries; Cypheme describes authentication technology; National Laboratory & Research describes testing; Tamarind Intelligence describes research. These observations are classifications, not endorsements or sales recommendations. Source links and limitations appear in the individual profiles.

**Conclusion:** comparing all entries as interchangeable manufacturers would be misleading. A brand, distributor, component business, laboratory and association represent different roles.

## 3. Two profile mismatches need explicit handling

- **Ayla / Tesiyi:** the organizer profile's narrative describes Wafoo and links to wafoovape.com. Preserve the listing, but do not assign Wafoo's corporate history or credentials to Ayla/Tesiyi.
- **LVL:** the organizer profile describes Aspire and links to aspirecig.com. This does not prove that LVL is Aspire.

Other names require caution: ELF & KEKO is not automatically ELFBAR; RELX in the combined RELX & WAKA entry must not be confused with the information-services company RELX. Short names such as Oplus, Weicheng and Mak Group have potential namesakes.

## 4. Brands and shared stands are not legal entities

Four stand numbers contain several website entries. Combined brand names also appear within single records. Neither arrangement proves common ownership, authorization or a manufacturing relationship.

Some company sources describe brand relationships, such as OXVA/OXBAR or Sikary/SKE. Preserve these as attributed company statements, not conclusions based on stand proximity. Review the underlying corporate documents before treating a brand relationship as a legal one.

## 5. The two lists disagree despite identical counts

The website and catalogue each have 138 entries, but only 136 normalized names overlap. A-Sense and FeelmixX appear only in the catalogue. Fox Electronics and THE GOLD LOTUS appear only on the website. Dinner Lady's location also conflicts.

**Conclusion:** a matching total is not evidence that two datasets match. The atlas retains the 140-name union and marks website membership. See [source reconciliation](source-reconciliation.md).

## 6. Six entries remain listing-only

Arik Nicotine Pouches, FENGDA, Global Wings International Logistics, Jingyaoyiqi, Mak Group LLC and THE GOLD LOTUS lack a sufficiently reliable operational description in this pass. Some names suggest an activity, but that is not verification. This is a research limitation, not an allegation about a company.

## 7. Appropriate next verification steps

For factual clarification, record the exact legal company name, registration jurisdiction, the representative's relationship to it and the source of each statement. Where safety or compliance matters, check current documents, their issuer, date, covered site and product scope. A badge, logo, exhibitor listing or company claim is not a substitute.

Do not infer approval from an FDA letter, MHRA notification, trademark filing, association membership or self-reported ISO/GMP claim. No certifications, product authorizations, financial condition, manufacturing capacity or beneficial ownership were audited in this project.
